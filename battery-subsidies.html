Battery subsidies and who enters

Many Chinese cities have tried to build a battery industry with local support programs. Which founders do these programs bring in, and what kinds of firms do they build?

Working paperGuankai Zhai and Chuck EesleyChina, 2009–2025

The questionSetting and dataApproachWhat we find

The question

Working paper

Local governments in China compete to grow strategic industries, and battery manufacturing has been a favorite target. Between 2009 and 2025, 56 of the country's 297 prefecture-level cities adopted programs to support it, 38 of them in 2022 and 2023 alone.

The programs share a goal but differ in their terms. Some offer benefits that any registered firm can claim, such as service vouchers or trade-fair subsidies. Others pay only after a firm reaches a production threshold or is selected for an industry fund.

What does support select on?

We ask how what a program requires a firm to show, before it can claim a benefit, shapes who enters the industry and what kinds of organizations they create. The answer matters for any government that uses entry subsidies to build a new industry.

What Support Selects On: Local Industrial Policy and Entrepreneurial Entry in China's Battery Industry. Guankai Zhai and Chuck Eesley, Department of Management Science & Engineering, Stanford University.

Setting and data

Cities
297 prefecture-level cities, 2009–2025
Programs
56 city programs, collected from government sources and dated
Firms
21,447 battery-manufacturing entities in China's business registry
Outcomes
New registrations, legal form, declared capital, survival and patents

Approach

  • Staggered difference-in-differences. Cities that adopted a program are compared with cities that never did, around each city's own adoption date.
  • Who enters, as well as how many. Each new registration is read for its legal form (sole proprietorship or limited-liability company), the capital it declares and the business it says it will do.
  • Comparison across programs. Programs are coded by what they require before a benefit is paid, to see whether the terms of support change the kind of entry that follows.

What we find

Early results point to more entry after a city adopts a program, with the newcomers tilted toward lower-commitment firms.

More entrants, and a different kind of entrant

Battery-sector registrations rise after adoption. A larger share of the new firms register as sole proprietorships with less declared capital, and fewer of them are still operating a year later.

Working paper. Results are preliminary and may change as the analysis develops.
