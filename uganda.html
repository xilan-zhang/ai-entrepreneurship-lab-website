Mentorship and entrepreneurial ideas

Entrepreneurs bring experience and ambitions to a new setting. Building a business also requires knowledge of local customers, suppliers and institutions.

The refugee entrepreneurship study in Uganda asks how mentorship shapes the ideas entrepreneurs develop. A randomized field experiment compares advice from experienced local Ugandan entrepreneurs with advice from fellow refugees. The research examines how knowledge of the host community and shared experience contribute to opportunity recognition.

The study combines evaluations of business ideas with surveys and qualitative interviews to understand how entrepreneurs interpret feedback and revise their plans.

In its 2023 feature, the Stanford King Center described a ten-week entrepreneurship course and the work of undergraduate researchers Natalie Milan and Faith Zehfuss. They helped adapt the teaching to local examples and connect participants with continuing support at Makerere University Business School.

AI advice and entrepreneurial judgment

Studies in preparation

AI makes business advice easier to obtain. Our next questions concern how entrepreneurs judge that advice and apply it to their own circumstances.

How should AI present alternative explanations?

When sales fall, an entrepreneur might consider pricing, customer demand or how the business reaches its market. A planned study with The Challenges Group Uganda examines whether presenting several possible causes separately helps entrepreneurs diagnose a problem more thoroughly than combining them into one recommendation.

The design compares a single AI perspective, several perspectives integrated into one recommendation, and the same alternatives kept separate.

How can AI work alongside human mentors?

A separate pilot under discussion with Challenges Uganda explores adding AI or digital tools to entrepreneurship support organized around village savings and loan associations. Human mentorship would remain available across groups. The question is whether additional digital support helps people turn advice into business actions and improve individual and group enterprises.

These are separate studies. Uganda fieldwork and the proposed pilot are being prepared; no Uganda AI impact results are reported here.
