Industrial policy and climate finance

When governments promise long-term support, does private capital follow? And does the design of the commitment matter?

The questionData and designFindingsPolicy credibilityInterpretation

The question

Working paper

Policies adopted today can be revised by the next legislature or administration. Investors may therefore discount even generous public incentives, and the investment those incentives target may never materialize.

We study this commitment problem in the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which directed about $369 billion toward climate and energy. Early-stage investors are a sharp test: their returns depend on a policy regime holding for a decade or more. We ask whether the IRA changed which climate technologies attract venture capital, how firms finance growth, and whether investors respond to the credibility of each commitment as well as its size.

Yikai Cao, Charles Eesley, Rishee Jain and Dinesh Moorjani · Stanford University and UCLA

Data and design

Firms
7,271 U.S. climate-technology companies from the Climate Tech VC (CTVC) universe
Panel
132,826 firm–quarters, Q1 2020–Q2 2025, with PitchBook deal records
Treated group
Energy and Carbon subfields, whose core technologies are targeted by 45X, 45V, 45Q, 50141 and 50144
Comparison group
Climate Management, Industrial, Food and Land Use, Built Environment and Transportation
  • Difference-in-differences and event studies with firm and time fixed effects and firm-clustered standard errors, using both a binary treatment and continuous exposure measures.
  • LLM-based exposure index. A large language model scores each firm's business description against the statutory language of each IRA provision (0–7); a text-similarity score provides a second, independent measure.
  • Financing trajectories. Financing events are classified into phases, and a four-state Hidden Markov Model fitted to 14,357 event sequences recovers financing regimes from the data.
  • Policy Credibility Index. Provision-level scores for specificity, durability and enforceability, built from the statutory text.

Findings

The IRA moved venture capital selectively, toward technologies most directly aligned with its incentives, and changed how climate firms finance growth.

Selective entry of venture capital

With firm and time fixed effects, exposure raises the quarterly probability of receiving VC by 0.9 percentage points (0.009, SE 0.002) and the number of venture deals by a similar amount. Pre-period estimates are small and indistinguishable from zero; effects emerge one to two quarters after enactment and persist.

The response is graded: a one-standard-deviation increase in text-based alignment corresponds to a 0.3 percentage-point higher probability of receiving VC and roughly $122,000–$147,000 more VC per firm-quarter.

Less venture debt, more venture-only and non-dilutive financing

Debt fell from 17.6% to 8.3% of financing events. The probability that a later-stage round is followed by debt halved (14.9% to 7.7%), while early-to-later-stage progression rose from 11.9% to 18.6%. The Hidden Markov Model recovers the same pattern: a pure-venture regime expands and a mixed venture–debt regime contracts.

Policy credibility

Investors price durability, and not every provision of the IRA is equally credible.

The Policy Credibility Index scores each provision on specificity, durability and enforceability (1–5 each). Two researchers scored provisions independently (over 85% agreement before reconciliation), and a blind re-scoring by LLM judges reproduces the ranking (ρ = 0.86). Self-executing tax credits score highest; discretionary loan programs score lowest.

Interpretation

  • Financing shares are computed over deal counts, not dollars. Interest rates also rose after 2022, and the time pattern alone cannot separate policy from the rate environment; this is why the shifts are read alongside the exposure-based designs.
  • Treated and control subfields may differ in baseline VC affinity. Firm fixed effects and continuous within-category exposure measures address this, and results hold when Transportation is reclassified as treated.
  • A news-based Climate Policy Uncertainty index co-moves with venture activity in the same direction; it is supporting evidence, not identification.
